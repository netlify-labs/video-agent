# Remix a video — plan

- **Status:** draft 2 (2026-11-03). Not built.
  - Draft 1 went to two independent remote reviews (Fable 5.1 high and Codex gpt-6.1-sol high, both "sound with changes"). Every claim they made was checked against the code before being folded in (§9).
  - Favorites, the other half of the same request, shipped in `abe1495`.
- **Ask (owner, 2026-11-03):** from a video, press **Remix**, say what should change, and get a new video. The new video starts from the original's composition, prompts and settings rather than from scratch: "reuse this, but change this, this and this". In effect, a clone plus a revision.
- **Builds on:** revisions (`reviseVideo` in `src/domain/videos.ts`, the `revision` run kind, `scripts/prepare-run.ts`), media snapshots (`version_media`), the contract's narration layer (`src/lib/contract.ts`), and the trust model in the README.

---

## 1. Do we already save what a remix needs? Yes

Every finished version keeps its composition. A remix needs no new kind of storage.

| What                                                                                                                       | Where                                                                                                    | Kept until                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The composition: `index.html`, `summary.json`, assets the agent made, AI voice-over clips (`assets/voice/`), music credits | `sources` store `{jobId}/v{n}.tar.gz` (≤ 10 MiB tar limit), hash in `outputs.sha256` (`finalize.ts:217`) | the video is deleted                                                                                     |
| What the archive leaves out                                                                                                | dot-paths, `node_modules`, `renders`, top-level MP4s, `feedback/`, library media, the narration bus      | (`bundle-manifest.ts:32-40`)                                                                             |
| A recorded narration bus                                                                                                   | `videos` store `{jobId}/v{n}/narration-bus.wav`, listed in `sources` `{jobId}/v{n}.references.json`      | the video is deleted                                                                                     |
| Library media the run used                                                                                                 | `version_media` rows (media key and hash)                                                                | while referenced: soft-deleting a library item keeps its bytes for versions that use it (`media.ts:373`) |
| Prompt, length, sound, AI voice-over settings, accepted plan                                                               | `versions` row                                                                                           | the video is deleted (soft delete; the rows stay)                                                        |

Edited (render-unchanged) versions write the same kind of `source.tar.gz` from their render, so their finished output can be remixed too.

A revision already restores a parent's archive into `video/` and asks the agent to change it. A remix is that run with three differences:

- The parent version is in **another video**.
- The result is a **new video**, owned by the remixer.
- Some of the parent's state must be **dropped**: recorded narration, edit state and captions.

## 2. Gotchas the design has to handle

1. **Every run check assumes one video.**
   - `verifyRunManifest` requires source keys under `${jobId}/` (`run-manifest.ts:308-310`).
   - `prepare-run.ts:478` rejects another job's manifest.
   - `readReferences` checks keys against the output's job (`bundles.ts:112`).
   - The reconciler finds the parent by `version.parentN` in the version's own job (`reconciler.ts:252-255`).
2. **Deleting the original queues deletion of its files.** `deleteVideo` tombstones the job at once and queues `delete_prefix` cleanups for `{jobId}/` (`videos.ts:640-649`). The next sweep deletes them. A remix that still pointed at the original's keys would fail its first run or a later **Try again** (`retryVersion` makes a new attempt that re-reads the source, `reconciler.ts:431-443`).
3. **A narrated version's composition still contains its narration**, even though the archive leaves out the bus file.
   - `index.html` keeps `<audio id="va-narration">` pointing at `assets/narration/bus.wav`.
   - The AI voice is muted or ducked under it.
   - `summary.json` keeps `narration: true` caption cues (`contract.ts:26-36`).
   - Restoring the archive without the bus gives a composition with a dangling audio element and stale captions. Telling the agent isn't enough: it has to be removed deterministically.
4. **Recorded narration is tied to its video.** Takes carry `media.scopeJobId`, and `media_refs` are per job. Narration is usually someone's own voice, and a remix usually retimes scenes, so it wouldn't fit anyway.
5. **Plans are tied to one video.** New videos need a plan with `jobId` null; revisions need the job's own plan (`videos.ts:112-115`).
6. **Comments, verdicts, publications and caption revisions** are about the original. None of them carry over.
7. **Cleanup rows are unconditional and unique per `(store, prefix)`.**
   - The sweep deletes any due row's prefix, with no notion of "the video exists" (`cleanup.ts:29-32`).
   - `insertCleanup` is `onConflictDoNothing` on `(store, prefix)` (`drizzle.ts:1192`).
   - So a remix's own cleanup row for `{newJobId}/` would either delete a remix that succeeded, or later swallow `deleteVideo`'s row, leaving the remix's files behind forever.
8. **Music is re-picked for versions without a parent.** `needsMusicPick` skips the Openverse pick only for a revision of a version with sound (`music-pick.ts:44-57`). A remix's v1 has `parentN` null, so it would get a second track on top of the one already in the archive.
9. **The title fallback matters.** Finalize replaces a video's title with the agent's only while it still equals `deriveTitle(v1.prompt)` (`finalize.ts:483`). A "{title} (remix)" default would block the agent's title.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Copy, clean and adopt the source into the new video

When a remix is created, `remixVideo`:

1. Checks the remixer can see the original (`canSee`) and that the version is `ready`. This happens **before** the idempotency lookup, as `reviseVideo` does (`videos.ts:391-392`).
2. Allocates the new job id. It writes a **reservation** cleanup row: store `sources`, prefix `{newJobId}/remix-source`, `nextActionAt` = now + 1 hour.
   - The prefix is narrower than `{newJobId}/`, so it never collides with `deleteVideo`'s row later (gotcha 7).
   - It cleans up after a crash or a lost race.
3. Reads the original's `source.tar.gz` (`BlobsPort` has only buffered `get`/`set`, which is fine within the 10 MiB tar limit). Verifies its hash, and refuses sources over the preview extraction limits (`tar.ts:12`).
4. **Cleans** the archive when the version is narrated (`version.narration` set, or `references.json` in its hashes):
   - Unpacks it in memory.
   - Removes the narration layer with `applyNarration(…, null)` (`contract.ts:789`). This must also restore a muted or ducked AI voice; a test proves it.
   - Drops `narration: true` cues from `summary.json`.
   - Validates the result with the contract checker, then repacks.
5. Writes the copy to `sources/{newJobId}/remix-source.tar.gz` and records its hash.
6. In **one transaction**:
   - Inserts the job, v1 and the attempt.
   - Snapshots `version_media`.
   - Records the idempotency key.
   - **Adopts** the copy by marking the reservation row done (`updateCleanup(id, { doneAt })`). `listDueCleanup` only takes rows with `doneAt` null.
   - If the transaction fails, or loses a concurrent race on the same idempotency key, the row stays open and the sweep removes the orphan copy an hour later.

From then on everything is under the new video's prefix:

- Every run-side check stays per-job and unchanged.
- **Try again** never touches the original.
- Deleting the remix removes the copy with everything else.

### 3.2 Runs: reuse the `revision` kind

Both reviews agree a new run kind buys little: `prepare-run` and `render-job` are already driven by `manifest.source`. The remix rules live on the server instead:

- **Reconciler** (`reconciler.ts:252`): add a branch before the `parentN` one. When `version.remix` is set, build the source from it (`key`, `sha256`, no references), never from `getOutput`.
  - The manifest's `source.parentN` is `null`. Widen its type in `run-manifest.ts`, the reconciler's local `revision` type (`:152`) and `buildPrompt`'s input (`prompt.ts:23`).
  - Omit `references` when there are none: an empty array is rejected (`run-manifest.ts:318`).
- **`parentSourceSha256`** on the attempt is the copy's hash.
- **`needsMusicPick`:** a remix whose source had sound keeps its music. Return false when `version.remix` is set and the source version's sound isn't `silent` (gotcha 8). `assets/music/credits.json` travels in the archive, and finalize rereads it (`finalize.ts:447`).
- **`prepare-run.ts:488-492`:** the "Restored video/ from v{n}" message reads "Restored video/ from the remixed video" when `parentN` is null.
- **Finalize** needs no change: caption carry-over only runs for frozen narration (`finalize.ts:242`), and editability (`contract`) is recomputed for the new v1 (`finalize.ts:280-282`).

### 3.3 Data

Migration `remix`:

- `jobs.remix_of_job_id text`, `jobs.remix_of_n integer`: provenance.
- `versions.remix jsonb` on a remix's v1, null otherwise. It holds everything a run or a retry needs, so nothing is re-read from the original:
  - `sourceKey`, `sourceSha256`: the cleaned copy.
  - `fromJobId`, `fromN`, `fromTitle`, `fromOwnerName`: display text for the prompt, snapshotted.
  - `history`: the original's prompt history up to `fromN`, oldest first, capped at 8 entries and 4,000 characters. For a remix of a remix, this is the remix's own history, which already starts with its source's.
  - `narrationDropped: boolean`.

### 3.4 What the remix carries over

| Copied (each one can be overridden in the remix form)                                                     | Not copied                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The finished composition (cleaned of recorded narration)                                                  | Recorded narration and its takes; `narration`, `captions` on the version                  |
| Aspect, language, length (`targetDurationSec`)                                                            | Edit state: `frozen`, `editedFromN`, `mode` (a remix's v1 is always `author`), `contract` |
| Sound and AI voice-over settings (their files are in the archive)                                         | Comments, verdicts, publications, notifications                                           |
| `version_media` rows, inherited as snapshots (deleted library items still render; new picks must be live) | The accepted `plan` (a remix can make its own plan from the new prompt)                   |
| Private flag (§5)                                                                                         | The original's webhook URL; author and API key (the remixer's)                            |

Changing the aspect is allowed, with a warning in the form. The agent re-lays out for the new frame, and finalize already checks the rendered dimensions (`finalize.ts:167`).

### 3.5 The agent's prompt

A remix's prompt is a revision prompt with a remix header. It is built only from `versions.remix`, so retries are identical:

- **Header:** "This is a remix of _{fromTitle}_ v{fromN} by {fromOwnerName}. `video/` holds its finished composition."
- **History:** the snapshotted history, labelled as background and fenced as quoted text.
- **Instructions:** "Make these changes: {remix prompt}. Keep everything else as it is."
- **Narration note:** when `narrationDropped` is set, add "Its recorded narration was removed; don't add a voice unless asked."
- **Title:** the job's title starts as `deriveTitle(remix prompt)`, exactly as `createVideo` does. The agent's title then replaces it at finalize (gotcha 9). There is no "(remix)" fallback.

`PROMPT_TEMPLATE_VERSION` goes up by one.

### 3.6 API, MCP, UI

- **API:** `POST /api/v1/videos/{id}/remix`.
  - Body: `{ fromVersion?, prompt, aspect?, language?, durationSec?, sound?, voiceover?, mediaIds?, planId? }`. `fromVersion` defaults to the latest ready version.
  - Takes an `idempotency-key` header, with scope `videos.remix:{sourceId}`, so the same key used on two originals conflicts instead of replaying.
  - Returns 202 with the new video and a `Location` header, then calls `kick`.
  - Added to `PAID_PATHS` (`app.ts:112`).
- **DTO:** `remixOf: { id, n, title } | null` on Video.
  - It's null when the original is deleted or the viewer can't see it.
  - `listVideos` preloads originals separately: `pageRepo.getJob` only finds jobs on the current page (`videos.ts:1084`).
  - Webhook (`'system'`) snapshots leave `remixOf` out.
- **MCP:** a `remix_video` tool mirroring `revise_video`. It is removed for roles without `video:create` (`mcp.ts:1484`) and named in the server instructions.
- **UI:**
  - A **Remix** button in the video page header, for Creators, on ready versions. It remixes the version being viewed.
  - It opens a dialog showing the original's prompt (collapsed), a "What should change?" box, and the revision controls (Sound, voice-over, length, aspect, media).
  - For a narrated version, the dialog notes "Your recorded narration isn't carried over."
  - **Remix** creates the video and goes to its page, which shows "Remix of _Title_ v3" under the title.
- **Mock server and OpenAPI:** the route and `remixOf` in `ui/src/mock`; `npm run openapi:gen`.

## 4. Permissions and credits

- Remixing needs `video:create` (Creators and Admins), and the original must be visible (`canSee`) and `ready`.
- Remixing someone else's video is allowed, as revising is today.
- Credits come from the team, as for every run. Usage is attributed to the remixer through v1's `authorId` and `apiKeyId`; there is no per-person ledger to change.
- **Trust model:** unchanged. Runs can already read any blob (README), and the server checks visibility before it copies.

## 5. Private originals

A private video is visible only to its owner and admins, so only they can remix it.

- The remix starts **private** when the original is, and gets its access mirror like `updateVideo` sets one (`videos.ts:605-613`, `files.ts:88`).
- **Known policy, stated plainly:** an admin who remixes someone's private video gets a copy of its content and prompt history, and can later make it public. Hiding the "Remix of" link can't hide copied content. That is the same trust model as admins seeing private videos today, and the owner can be told (§6, question 3).

## 6. Decisions and open questions

Decided, because both reviews agree:

1. **Aspect changes:** allowed, with a warning.
2. **Counts and links on the original:** deferred. v1 ships "Remix of" on the new video only.
3. **Recorded narration:** dropped in v1 and removed deterministically (§3.1, step 4), with a note in the dialog. Keeping your own voice can be an explicit option later.

Open, for the owner:

4. **Should the original's owner be notified when someone remixes their video?**
   - Fable: yes. `notifyOwner` already skips self-actions, and it tells owners when an admin copies a private video.
   - Codex: defer, to keep remix creation free of another workflow.
   - Recommendation: yes, but only for private originals or remixes by someone else. It's a single best-effort insert after the commit.

## 7. Build order

1. **Data and runs:**
   - The migration and the `versions.remix` type.
   - The reconciler branch, the `parentN: number | null` widening (run manifest, reconciler, prompt), and omitting empty references.
   - `needsMusicPick` and the prepare-run message.
   - Tests on the memory store and PGlite:
     - A remix run restores the copy.
     - Try again after the original is deleted still works.
     - A music-picked source gets no second pick.
2. **Domain:**
   - `remixVideo`: visibility before idempotency, reservation, copy, narration clean, adoption in the transaction, settings and `version_media` copy, private flag and mirror.
   - The remix prompt.
   - Tests:
     - Permissions, a private original, a version that isn't ready, overrides.
     - A narrated source: no `va-narration`, AI voice unmuted, no narration cues, contract valid.
     - An edited source.
     - A remix of a remix.
     - Deleted library media.
     - An oversized source refused.
     - Two concurrent remixes with one key on PGlite: one video, and the loser's copy is swept.
     - A failed transaction leaves an open reservation that the sweep clears.
     - A crash after the copy is cleaned within the hour.
3. **API, MCP and OpenAPI:**
   - The route, `PAID_PATHS`, `remixOf` with the page preload and the webhook redaction, and the MCP tool with the role filter.
   - The mock routes, and the production-only guard kept (`app.ts:200`).
4. **UI:** the Remix dialog, the narration note and the "Remix of" link. Check them in the mock build, in both themes, and in Playwright.
5. **End to end:** `scripts/e2e/remix.ts` against production. It remixes a ready video with a one-line change and checks that the new video's first frame still looks like the original, with the change applied. It also remixes a narrated video and checks the narration is gone.

## 8. Not in v1

- A remix count and reverse links on the original.
- Keeping recorded narration in a remix.
- Remixing several versions or videos into one.
- Streaming blob copies (the 10 MiB tar limit keeps buffering cheap).
